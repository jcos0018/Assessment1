Section 2: Research question

What is the trend in Australia’s population growth and median age over time? What is the relationship between median age and population growth across various countries?

Section 3: Dataset introduction

The dataset used for this assignment is age and population data for selected countries, from 1950 to 2023. This dataset is a subset of a larger dataset (containing all countries, and more population and migration variables), available from the World Population Prospects 2024 website. The specific excel file is linked here..

Due to dataset restrictions for this assignment (1000 records), only 13 countries were selected for use in the final dataset. Australia, the United States, the United Kingdom, plus the ten largest countries in the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific (ESCAP). The United States and United Kingdom were selected as comparable first world democracies to Australia, while the ESPAC countries were chosen due to being geographically in a similar economic region as Australia.

The final dataset includes 5 variables: Country name; Year; Population size; Population growth rate; and median age.

Section 7: Conclusion

Australia’s median age has been increasing over time, at least since 1950. Meanwhile, Australia’s annual rate of population growth has declined overall since 1950, although has fluctuated over this time. These trends are broadly reflected in other countries in Asia-Pacific region, and for similar first-world economies including the United States and the United Kingdom.

There is a strong negative relationship between the median age of a country, and its annual population growth. That is, the higher the median age of a country, the lower its annual population growth is likely to be on average.

These trends suggest that many countries, Australia included, are facing the challenges of an ageing population, and that declining population growth rates may be exacerbating these challenges as there may be insufficient people to support the economy and look after the elderly. Japan stands out as an outlier in the data with a particularly high median age, and negative population growth rate in recent years. According to the World Economic Forum, these challenges are already having significant impacts on many facets of Japanese society.

These conclusions are based on analysis of data from 13 countries including Australia. To strengthen these findings, further analysis could be conducted on a wider array of countries from various continents. In addition, breakdowns of population growth by natural increases versus migration-related increases would add further insight.
